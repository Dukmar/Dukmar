# Pitch IQ research notes

Researched 2026-10-22 for a 10-year-old girl playing 7v7 in Halton Region, Ontario.

**How solid is this?** This cloud session's network policy blocked every rules page and PDF (ontariosoccer.net, canadasoccer.com, oakvillesoccer.ca, burlingtonsoccer.com, the app stores). Everything below comes from search-engine results that quote those pages. Where several independent sources agreed, I treated the point as settled enough to teach. Where they disagreed, the app either avoids the point or says "your league decides". The items under **Check before you trust it** are the ones to confirm against your club's current rule sheet.

## 1. Halton / Ontario 7v7 rules (U10–U11)

|---|---|---|

### Check before you trust it
1. Which format her club uses at her age (Burlington moves U11 to 9v9).
2. The kick-in distance (3 m vs 2 yd) and whether her league uses dribble-ins at all.
3. Whether free kicks are all indirect in her league.
4. Whether her keeper may punt or drop-kick.
5. The formation her coach actually uses. The app teaches 2-3-1 first and 3-2-1 second.

## 2. Soccer IQ curriculum used in the app

Standard coaching content (Canada Soccer pathway, US Soccer grassroots, The FA's principles of play) cross-checked through search results. Sources: [The FA: principles of play](https://learn.englandfootball.com/articles/resources/2022/What-are-the-principles-of-football), [US Soccer 7v7 formations](https://www.ussoccer.com/us-way/player-development/game/formations/u9-u10-7v7), [US Soccer game model U9–U10](https://www.ussoccer.com/us-way/player-development/game/game-models/u9-u10), [Jordet scanning research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7573254), [Canada Soccer pathway guide](https://abbotsfordsoccer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CanadaSoccerPathway-LTPD-Community-Guide.pdf).

- **Formations.** Numbers run back to front and skip the keeper. 2-3-1 (2 defenders, 2 wingers + centre mid, 1 striker) is the common youth starting point because it makes triangles everywhere. 3-2-1 is safer at the back. No source names one "official" U10 formation; US Soccer's chart shows 3-2-1 and says its charts are examples only.
- **Attacking principles:** penetration, support, width, depth, mobility, creativity.
- **Defending principles:** pressure (first defender), cover (second), balance (third), compactness, delay, recovery runs, goal-side.
- **Without the ball, we have it:** get open (a clear passing lane), make triangles, stay wide, keep depth, check away then come back, don't bunch.
- **Without the ball, they have it:** nearest player presses, the next one covers, everyone else tucks in toward the middle and stays goal-side; recovery runs go toward your own goal, not the ball. 1v1: side-on, close but don't dive in, show her to the sideline.
- **With the ball:** scan before it arrives (players who look around more complete more passes in Jordet's studies, a link rather than proof), first touch away from pressure, open body, pass/dribble/shoot decision, pass to feet if marked and into space if she's running, switch play, shield.
- **Transitions.** Win it: look forward first. Lose it: nearest player presses right away, the rest recover. The "5-second" counter-press is a rule of thumb (sources say 5–8 s). US Soccer adds transition as a separate topic from U11, so the app keeps it simple.
- **Goalkeeper:** stay on the line between the ball and the middle of the goal, step out to narrow the angle, be set before the shot, talk, roll or throw to a defender out wide.
- **Communication:** "Man on!", "Time!", "Turn!", "Switch!", "Keeper!", "Away!", "Drop!", "Push up!" plus a name first. Sources define the calls slightly differently; the app uses one set consistently.
- **Leadership and fair play:** positive talk, body language after mistakes, organising the shape, helping teammates up, accepting the ref's call, kicking the ball out for an injury.
- **Extras a newcomer wouldn't think of:** thirds of the field, the weak side, 2v1 overloads, give-and-go, overlap, play to the whistle, stay on your feet, quick restarts, ball watching, rotating positions.

### Teaching a 10-year-old girl
- Canada Soccer places girls U8–U11 in "Learning to Train", the "golden age of learning": fun but demanding, learning by doing.
- Praise effort and improvement; specific praise beats general praise.
- Girls drop out of sport at 3× the rate of boys by late teens (Canadian Women & Sport, Rally Report 2020); appearance comments are a stated reason. The app has no body or appearance content and no leaderboards against other kids.
- One idea per card, show it then try it, short sessions.

**The gap Pitch IQ fills:** nothing found is built for 7v7, nothing lets a kid drag herself around a 3D pitch and get told whether she's in the right spot, and nothing is designed for girls. Borrowed ideas: position-puzzle levels (TactiQuest), a first-person "her eyes" camera (Tactic3D), freeze-pick-see-why decisions (Train Effective's Game Brain), short sessions with streaks (IntelliGym).
