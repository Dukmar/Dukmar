## 1. Halton / Ontario 7v7 rules (U10–U11)

"Ontario" below means the April 2026 adapted laws.

| Rule | What the documents say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who plays | 7v7 including keeper at U10 and U11 (Ontario, Peel Halton, Oakville). | Ontario, PHSA, Oakville |
| **Burlington exception** | House league: U9 and U10 play 7v7; **U11 and U12 play 9v9** on a 50 × 70 m field with throw-ins. | BSC 2025 |
| Field | 30–36 m wide × 40–55 m long. Penalty area 20–24 m wide × 10–12 m deep. **No separate goal area** ("the goal area is the same as the penalty area"). Corner area radius 0.5 m. Optional 7 m penalty arc. | Ontario, PHSA, Oakville |
| Goal / ball | 4.8 × 1.8 m (16 × 6 ft); size 4 ball. | Ontario, PHSA |
| Game length | 2 × 25 min or 4 × 12.5 min, 5-min half-time. | Ontario, Oakville |
| Subs / playing time | Unlimited, at any stoppage (Ontario). Oakville: equal playing time and every player rotates through every position. Burlington: equal time. | Ontario, Oakville, BSC |
| Offside | **None** at 7v7. | Ontario, PHSA, Oakville |
| Retreat line | **One-third line.** Applies on goal kicks, when the keeper has the ball in her hands, and on free kicks in a team's own third. Opponents stay behind it until a teammate of the keeper touches the ball, it leaves the field, or it crosses the line. If the keeper releases it before they retreat, play is live. After a retreat the keeper may dribble (opponents can press after her second touch), throw or pass. | Ontario, ontariosoccer.net, PHSA, Oakville |
| Ball over the touchline | **No throw-ins.** Dribble-in or pass-in from where it went out; ball stationary; opponents **at least 3 m** away; no goal straight from it. | Ontario Law 15, PHSA, Oakville |
| Kick-off | Everyone in own half, opponents 5 m back. A goal may be scored directly. | Ontario, Oakville |
| Free kicks | **All indirect except penalty kicks.** Opponents 5 m back. With a wall of 3+ defenders, attackers stay 1 m from it. | Ontario Law 13, Oakville |
| Goal kicks | From anywhere in the penalty area; retreat line applies. Ontario: a goal may be scored directly. **Oakville:** no goal directly from a goal kick, and only the keeper takes goal kicks. | Ontario Law 16, Oakville |
| Corners | From the corner area; opponents 5 m back. | Ontario Law 17 |
| Penalty kicks | Fouls A–L (kicking, tripping, pushing, holding, handball...) inside the box. Mark 8 m from goal (Ontario). **Oakville's 2024 summer sheet says 9 m from the goal line.** | Ontario, Oakville summer 2024 |
| Keeper's hands | Indirect free kick if she handles a deliberate kick back from a teammate, or a ball straight from a teammate's kick-in. | Ontario Law 12 |
| Keeper kicks | Ontario: "It is recommended that the goalkeeper does not use a dropkick." **Oakville: drop kicks are permissible**, but keepers are encouraged to play out from the back; no 6-second rule. | Ontario, Oakville |
| Heading | Canada Soccer: heading "should not be introduced in training sessions" at U10–U11 and "remains rare in games"; coaches "should encourage short corners and free kicks". | Canada Soccer 2025 |
| Scores | None kept (Oakville, Burlington, PHSA, per Ontario LTPD). | Oakville, BSC, PHSA |
| Referee | One referee, no assistants; small-sided referees avoid yellow and red cards for players and explain fouls. | Ontario Laws 5, 6, 12 |
| Who runs it | Inter-club grassroots play: Peel Halton Soccer Association. House league: each club. PHSA runs female divisions at U10–U12. | PHSA 2026 |

## 2. Soccer IQ curriculum used in the app

Standard coaching content (Canada Soccer pathway, US Soccer grassroots, The FA's principles of play) cross-checked through search results. Sources: [The FA: principles of play](https://learn.englandfootball.com/articles/resources/2022/What-are-the-principles-of-football), [US Soccer 7v7 formations](https://www.ussoccer.com/us-way/player-development/game/formations/u9-u10-7v7), [US Soccer game model U9–U10](https://www.ussoccer.com/us-way/player-development/game/game-models/u9-u10), [Jordet scanning research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7573254), [Canada Soccer pathway guide](https://abbotsfordsoccer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CanadaSoccerPathway-LTPD-Community-Guide.pdf).

- **Formations.** Numbers run back to front and skip the keeper. 2-3-1 (2 defenders, 2 wingers + centre mid, 1 striker) is the common youth starting point because it makes triangles everywhere. 3-2-1 is safer at the back. No source names one "official" U10 formation; US Soccer's chart shows 3-2-1 and says its charts are examples only.
- **Attacking principles:** penetration, support, width, depth, mobility, creativity.
- **Defending principles:** pressure (first defender), cover (second), balance (third), compactness, delay, recovery runs, goal-side.
- **Without the ball, we have it:** get open (a clear passing lane), make triangles, stay wide, keep depth, check away then come back, don't bunch.
- **Without the ball, they have it:** nearest player presses, the next one covers, everyone else tucks in toward the middle and stays goal-side; recovery runs go toward your own goal, not the ball. 1v1: side-on, close but don't dive in, show her to the sideline.
- **With the ball:** scan before it arrives (players who look around more complete more passes in Jordet's studies, a link rather than proof), first touch away from pressure, open body, pass/dribble/shoot decision, pass to feet if marked and into space if she's running, switch play, shield.
- **Transitions.** Win it: look forward first. Lose it: nearest player presses right away, the rest recover. The "5-second" counter-press is a rule of thumb (sources say 5–8 s). US Soccer adds transition as a separate topic from U11, so the app keeps it simple.
- **Goalkeeper:** stay on the line between the ball and the middle of the goal, step out to narrow the angle, be set before the shot, talk, roll or throw to a defender out wide.
- **Communication:** "Man on!", "Time!", "Turn!", "Switch!", "Keeper!", "Away!", "Drop!", "Push up!" plus a name first. Sources define the calls slightly differently; the app uses one set consistently.
- **Leadership and fair play:** positive talk, body language after mistakes, organising the shape, helping teammates up, accepting the ref's call, kicking the ball out for an injury.
- **Extras a newcomer wouldn't think of:** thirds of the field, the weak side, 2v1 overloads, give-and-go, overlap, play to the whistle, stay on your feet, quick restarts, ball watching, rotating positions.

### Teaching a 10-year-old girl
- Canada Soccer places girls U8–U11 in "Learning to Train", the "golden age of learning": fun but demanding, learning by doing.
- Praise effort and improvement; specific praise beats general praise.
- Girls drop out of sport at 3× the rate of boys by late teens (Canadian Women & Sport, Rally Report 2020); appearance comments are a stated reason. The app has no body or appearance content and no leaderboards against other kids.
- One idea per card, show it then try it, short sessions.

**The gap Pitch IQ fills:** nothing found is built for 7v7, nothing lets a kid drag herself around a 3D pitch and get told whether she's in the right spot, and nothing is designed for girls. Borrowed ideas: position-puzzle levels (TactiQuest), a first-person "her eyes" camera (Football 3D Viewer), short sessions with streaks (IntelliGym). The "freeze, pick, see why" decision game is my own; Train Effective's Game Brain explains decisions but doesn't quiz them.

**Scanning research check:** Jordet and colleagues (Frontiers in Psychology, 2020) followed 27 players at one Premier League club over 21 matches and 9,574 possessions: "the more a player scans, the higher the probability of completing a pass", an effect they call "small, but positive". It's a correlation in elite players, which is how the app words it. [Paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.553813/full)
